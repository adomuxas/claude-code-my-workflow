Title: New GDP series for interwar and Soviet Estonia: a Finnish miracle that never was
The main value-added of the paper is the provision of the first reliable and internationally comparable Estonian GDP per capita series for 1919-1995 and, after splicing with existing 1913 figure from Norkus and Markeviciute and modern data in the Maddison project database, provision of the full uninterrupted 1913/1919-2022 GDP per capita PPP series in international 1990 and 2011 dollars which can be readily incorporated into the Maddison Project Database.
The paper should briefly describe the estimation procedures which are three-dimensional: the first dimension covers the Soviet occupation period data which is based on the 1990 benchmark by Bolotin and extends the series to 1937-1995. The second dimension should criticise the existing Estonian GDP per capita PPP figures from Valge 2003 and provide a new 1938 GDP per capita PPP benchmark for reconstructing the Estonian 1919-1940 GDP per capita figures in an analogous way as done for Lithuania and Latvia in the already published papers by Klimantas and colleagues. The third dimension is using the new 1938 benchmark to complement and recalculate Valge's figures and extend them to 1919-1940.
The paper should then describe splicing the series together, including with the post-1990 data from Maddison project database, giving a table with the overlap in 1937-1940 and 1990-1995.
The paper should then provide the final 1913/1919-2022 series in 1990 and 2011 international dollars as well as Estonian population figures for the respective period.
The paper should then utilise the data to tell a short new story of Estonian comparative economic growth during the interwar and Soviet periods, comparing with previous estimates and telling a new story covering the pre-WWI era, WWI impact, interwar crises and developments, WWII impact, growth and stagnation under Soviet occupation and the actual decline in GDP per capita during the transition to market economy afterwards. This discussion should be explaining newly discovered Estonian growth trends using the literature contained in the Papers folder.
Finally, the paper should additionally offer an interesting utilisation of the data to intrigue the reader. In many of the Source papers dealing with Estonian economic growth issue you'll find recurring comparisons with Finland as Estonia's "alter ego" before Soviet occupation and the emergence of Estonia quite well below the Finnish level in the 1990s being evidence for negative impact of soviet occupation since Finland had experienced a "growth miracle" during the cold war. The paper can discuss this issue at first and then, after providing the estimates, systematically compare the country with Finland using the various other data contained in the Datasheets folder. The paper may conclude that while the divergence between Finland and Estonia expands during the Soviet era, the differences during the interwar period are too great to imply that Finland serves as the Estonia's counterfactual.
The paper should also contain a section on the limitations and sensitivity analysis of the new estimates, covering the single deflation issues, late benchmark issues etc.
