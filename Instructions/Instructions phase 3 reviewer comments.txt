Reviewer comments on the “3 LT Klimantas 2026 Accounting for a Soviet republic”, reviewer comments inside quotation marks, my suggestions for edits for you to make afterwards.
1.	“using sectoral weights for a single year (1990) to reconstruct GDP dynamics for more than a fifty-year period (1938-1995), which saw substantial structural change, is problematic. Such an approach likely underestimates growth dynamics because of the so-called Gershenkron effect: the late-weighted (Paasche) index is biased upward because it assigns lower weights to rapidly growing sectors (goods produced in these sectors became cheaper relative to the starting years due to the rapid growth of these sectors associated with structural change).” – Since I will not be recalculating anything, I need to make a very strong case why my chosen approach is the best and the only feasible option which actually shows realistic estimates.
2.	“how well the reconstructed series fits the estimates for the post-1990 and pre-1940 is discussed only briefly in the current version of the paper. Table 2 shows almost a ten percent difference for 1938 and a fifteen percent difference for 1990 with earlier reconstructions; this is a lot. This raises the question of how the switch from 1990 planned prices in rubbles to 1990 market prices in USD is done.” – I need to strongly argue that the 1938-1940 and 1990-1995 overlap is good enough.
3.	“The Bolotin's (1990) benchmark is taken as given, and its quality is not discussed. This is particularly pitiful given research by Ponomarenko (2002) for Russia and Kim (2003) for other Soviet republics, who, in contrast to Bolotin, extensively use data from the former Soviet archives.” – Kim is not using any archival data for national income reconstructions, so we don’t need to deal with that point. But Ponomarenko’s may be a stronger case I’ve included this work in the Previous research papers folder for you to analyse. Ideally I need to make a solid case on why my approach is the best feasible and similar in quality to Ponomarenko. In any case, I won’t be looking for a new benchmark, so I also need to strengthen the case that Bolotin’s 1990 benchmark is optimal and I also need to discuss the previous research on Soviet historical accounts which would include Ponomarenko’s work.
4.	“The analysis of the reconstructed series is also shallow and includes many bold claims. What is special about the Lithuanian growth story? Does the Lithuanian series simply follow the Soviet macro dynamics, or does it differ from the whole Union story, and if yes, how? My brief inspection of the reconstructed series gives at least two interesting descriptive evidence worth discussing and possibly organizing the whole narrative of the paper around them. (1) Lithuania grew faster than the rest of the Soviet Union, especially in the early years of the period; Lithuanian GDP per capita was lower than the Soviet average before WWII and higher than the average on the eve of the collapse of the Soviet system. Why? Was it Moscow's attempt to buy loyalty? The paper discusses Finland as a counterfactual growth path, but is this the right comparison, given the difference in initial conditions (Finland was already richer than the Soviet Union in 1940) and culture (Catholics vs. protestants)? (2) The Agricultural sector stagnated between 1938 and 1958 (with a drop during WWII and a slow recovery after, despite collectivization). There was nothing comparable to the 1932-3 disaster associated with collectivization in the rest of the Soviet Union. Does it mean that the planners learned the lesson?” – a comparison with the USSR makes sense. Include it somewhere in the implications part, showing that Estonia was absorbed into the USSR with a much higher income level that the Soviet total figure and see how the divergence shifts through the years under occupation to suggest whether there was a shift towards convergence or whether Estonia remained one of the richest Soviet parts with much higher productivity by the end of the Soviet era. Finland is the right comparator for Estonia, the paper well establishes that. Some mention of collectivization impact based on the sectoral output data makes sense – do that. 
5.	“does the author use only officially published Soviet data? Or archival series as well? Are formerly classified products (military and related) included? Also, what is the quality of the sources for the Nazi period?” – I need to include a discussion on this, particularly underscoring that archival sources were very much used.
6.	“the analysis should be replicated with the 1938 sectoral weights. Also, the assumption "of unchanging constant-price value-added-to-output ratios (single deflation) from 1937 to 1995" most likely does not hold, and potential biases should be at least discussed.” – let’s not do the replication with 1938 weights but the paper must discuss the issue of unchanging value-added to output ratios and must clearly assess the bias in the sensitivity analysis of the limitations section.
7.	“There is also a methodological problem of accounting for transfers between republics in the Soviet Union. Similarly, should Soviet defence expenditures be included?” – No need to discuss this in the paper for now.
8.	“Figure 5 demonstrates the rapid growth of the textile sector up to 1974 and then stagnation, even relative to other industrial sectors. The reader would benefit from a discussion of such dynamics. Similarly, construction shocks in 1956, 1962, and 1979 are very intriguing because the central planner usually did her best to avoid them. Also, the decline of the housing sector already in 1986 requires explanation (Figure 6).” – See if you can spot irregularities in Estonian sectoral volume index growth and try to explain them using the papers in the papers for background and implications sections folders.

See review by Zhuravskaya et al (2024).” – this recent paper must be mentioned when discussing the state of research into Soviet economic developments which should also be discussed. The Zhuravskaya et al (2024) is included in the “Important papers for background and implications sections” folder
