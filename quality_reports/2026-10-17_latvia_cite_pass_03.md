## (c) Qualifications added

1. §3.1, one sentence (≈40 words) after the comparison of the official and physical industrial indices: the official index rests on a 1940 base covering only the five months of Soviet rule, valued at an arbitrary lat–rouble parity, and its 1947 claim is contradicted by the physical series (M&T 109; Krūmiņš 2017, 234; Terleckas 2022, 290, 296).
2. §3.2, footnote on the turnover tax falling on final consumption goods and accruing to the republic of final manufacture (Yasin 1993, 20): explains why the incidence of product taxes by branch and republic is uneven and why no adjusted-factor-cost re-pricing was possible.
3. §3.2, footnote after "Third, there is no alternative": the range of earlier claims (Fedotov 1988 >70 % of the US; PlanEcon/CIA Estonia 1989 USD 6,240 vs USSR 5,000) (Kukk 2005, 166).
4. §3.5, footnote registering Bukowski, Kowalski and Wroński's objection that the interwar extrapolation holds relative prices at their 1935 structure, and identifying it as the index-number problem treated in the sensitivity subsection (Bukowski et al. 2026, 6). The method description is not changed.
5. §3.5, attributive clause "as contemporaries and later historians have judged it" so that the two new citations (Norkus 2014, 322–3; Krūmiņš 2024, 44) support the characterisation, not the paper's own 37 per cent figure.
6. §3.7, clause: the EBRD index also rises in 1989 (+6.8 %) and 1990 (+2.9 %), whereas Krūmiņš's physical series put 1990 output below 1985 (Kyn 1997, 269; Krūmiņš 2017, 348–50). Other authors' series noted; the paper's claim unchanged.
7. §3.8, clause: Latvian kolkhoz-market price index rose 4 %/yr in 1970–5 and 13 % in 1975–7 "while officially there was no inflation" (M&T 221), as Latvia-specific evidence for the hidden-inflation bias in the trade index.
8. §3.8, clause: the World Bank's count of some 300,000 Soviet military personnel and dependants (11 % of population) at the time of its 1993 report (World Bank 1993 housing, 157), beside the paper's 80,000 troops in 1990. The paper's figure is unchanged.

## (d) Not resolved (looked for support, none inserted)

- "some 80,000 Soviet troops were stationed in Latvia in 1990, far more in proportion to population than in Lithuania or Estonia" (§3.3, §3.8): no page in the corpus gives 80,000 or a Baltic ranking of garrisons. World Bank 1993 (p. 157) gives 300,000 including dependants (added as a qualification); Demuth 2000 (p. 19) gives only a Baltic-wide share ("10% of all Soviet troops"); Grybkauskas 2011 (p. 205) calls Latvia "the most militarised Baltic republic" but in a passage about industry and immigration, so it was not used for the garrison. Left as is.
- Date of the Abrene transfer (paper: 1944): Misiunas and Taagepera (p. 74) describe it in a paragraph on cessions "in January 1945" (Estonia) without dating the Latvian case; Hiden and Salmon give no date. The paper's 1944 was not changed.
- "the manipulation of earlier returns having been publicly acknowledged by Baltic statisticians in 1988" (`adlys1988`) and "the reporting of 1989--90 … was more transparent than that of any earlier Soviet year": no corroborating page in the corpus; Pihlamägi 2013 (p. 252) concerns the embellished 1965 plan-fulfilment statistics, not 1988. Not cited.
- The *Statistische Berichte für das Ostland* / *Ostland in Zahlen* (§3.3, §3.3 war years): Grėska 1962 (pp. 9, 11–13) cites the Riga issues Nr. 1/2 1942, 1/4 1943 and 1/4 1944, which confirms the publication, but no key for Grėska exists in `all_bibkeys.txt`; not cited.
- `olaf_stalin` (Mertelsmann, Stalinist restructuring) p. 53 on the conversion of 1940 output into 1926/27 roubles ("A similar method was obviously also used in Latvia and Lithuania"): the folder is the 2022 manuscript and the catalogued edition is the 2024 printed book; pagination unconfirmed, so not cited. The point is covered instead by Mertelsmann 2003 (165–7), M&T 109, Krūmiņš 234 and Terleckas 290.
- Mertelsmann 2003 (pp. 167–9) argues that Estonian (and by assertion Latvian) industry did not regain pre-war output until the second half of the 1950s; this bears on the recovery dating in Section 4, not on Section 3, and was left for that section's pass. Vaškela 2014 (p. 229) was used only for the narrower point that the official 1947 claim is disputed.
- The Maddison Project 1990 levels quoted in §3.6 (15,806 and 9,916) are confirmed by Norkus 2023 (p. 161) and Norkus 2014 (p. 73), but the primary database is already cited and no secondary confirmation was added.
- Statistical-yearbook coverage "from 1957" and the incompleteness of early post-war data (§3.3): Vaškela 2014 (p. 228) and Truska 2000 (p. 458) speak of Lithuania and of the 1959 census, not of the Latvian yearbooks; not cited.
- Kukk 2005 (p. 169) EIU PPP GDP per head for Latvia 1989–97 and van Selm 1997 (p. 35) 1988 capital stock and investment per head: relevant to Table 4 (previous estimates, generated by R) and to Sections 4–5, not to any sentence in Section 3; not inserted.
- Daviddi and Espa 1995 (p. 31: Latvia retained 43 % of turnover tax; grant of R278 mn in 1986–7): a budget-retention figure, not the branch incidence Section 3 discusses; not inserted.
