## (c) Qualifications added (all as footnotes; the paper's own figures are untouched)

1. Industry regained 1938 level (\sregain{Industry}): Misiunas and Taagepera agree (recovery by 1949, p. 113); Mertelsmann's contrary view (Estonian recovery only in the second half of the 1950s; Latvia assumed similar, pp. 167–9) registered.
2. Income per head above 1938 by 1950: consumption lagged output; urban families in 1961 consumed 30–50 per cent less meat, eggs, dairy than pre-war; pre-war living standards commonly dated to the 1960s (M&T pp. 185–6; Puur et al. p. 38).
3. Net-contributor finding (18 per cent retained by the Union): Aage's implicit-transfer (cheap energy) caveat, p. 102.
4. March 1949 deportation count: archival 41,445 (Feest) vs older 50,000+ (Labsvirs, M&T), and date 25 March (Strods, Pocius).
5. Collectivisation share at end-1949: 93 per cent also in M&T p. 102 and Indrišionis p. 280; Terleckas's 90 per cent on 1 January 1950 (p. 225) sits between the paper's two counts.
6. 1990 output movement and the 1989 Estonia–Latvia overtaking: official/EBRD series show Latvian GDP +2.9 per cent in 1990 (Kyn p. 269; Kukk p. 13), and official income per head still placed Latvia above Estonia in 1988–9 (van Selm p. 35; Norkus p. 156). Paper's claim (own series) unchanged.
7. Depth of the 1989–93 fall: official GNP −48 per cent 1990–93 (Aage p. 96); EBRD 1994 = 52 per cent of 1989 (Kukk p. 13). Paper's per-head figure unchanged.

Plus one supporting (non-qualifying) footnote on the mechanics of the 1959 purge (Smith, Strods, Grybkauskas).

## (d) Not resolved / deliberately not acted on

- "Latvians … 52 per cent in 1989": Hiden and Salmon (hiden_salmon p. 132) give 50.7 per cent; the census figure of 52.0 per cent is confirmed by Puur et al. (Table 2.2) and the paper's existing sources, so no change and no Hiden–Salmon citation.
- "savings above 1,000 lats annulled" (March 1941): Terleckas 2000 p. 365 (per the batch-05 sheet) says Latvia limited deposit withdrawals to 100 roubles in autumn 1940. This is a different measure (1940 withdrawal limit, not the 1941 annulment); page not opened, nothing changed.
- "All-Union ministries controlled between a third and two-fifths of industrial output": Hiden and Salmon's "almost 90 per cent … run solely or jointly by All-Union authorities" (p. 171) is a different measure (includes Union-republican); M&T p. 228 (34 per cent Union ministries in 1971) would confirm the paper's figure but the sentence already carries three sources. Not added.
- "in 1970--85 its growth was the lowest of any Soviet republic": M&T p. 235 confirms only that Latvian industrial growth fell below the Soviet average in the 1970s; the republic ranking rests solely on Krūmiņš p. 304. Cited M&T as partial confirmation; the ranking itself remains single-sourced.
- "privatisation … completed largely in 1996--7": Jeffries pp. 208–9 confirms the slow start (85 of 698 by April 1994) and that fewer than 200 objects remained by 1997–8, but does not state "1996–7" verbatim; Bitāns p. 142 remains the main support for "slower than Estonia and Lithuania". The batch-00 sheet notes Lane (lane_2001_lithuania p. 164) finds Lithuania's transition "slightly slower than Latvia's"; left as is.
- 1962–3 agricultural crisis: no Latvia-specific source in the corpus beyond Krūmiņš p. 299 (May 1962 price rise) and Purre 1964 (already cited); the Union-wide account still rests on Belkharoev and Andreenkov.
- "the 1989 level of income per head was regained in \lvREGAINeightynine{}, after Estonia and Lithuania": Norkus (norkus_2023_baltic p. 164, per the sheet) gives MPD-based years Estonia 2000, Lithuania 2004, Latvia 2005; the ordering agrees with the paper, so no qualification was added and the page was not opened.
- King's "over 1,000 industrial enterprises (85% of productive capacity)" vs the paper's "1,477 enterprises producing 84 per cent" (Krūmiņš): different counts, Krūmiņš archival; no action.
- Items flagged in the brief but located in other sections (Estonian 1949 deportation count 60,000 in M&T vs 20,660 in Feest; "every physical indicator was already falling" in 02_gap; Lithuanian fall deeper on official series in 05_baltic) were not touched because they are outside 04_latvia.tex.
