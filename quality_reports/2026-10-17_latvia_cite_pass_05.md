## (b) Corrections made

**C1. Latvian literacy vs Finnish (L47).** Flagged item; verified on `norkus_2014_…_lietuva_sparciau_turtejo/p0103.txt` (Table 1.7, Darbiņš & Vītiņš 1947): Finland 0.9 per cent illiterate (1930, age 15+), Estonia 3.9 (1934, 10+), Latvia 11.1 (1935, 10+), Italy 20.9, Lithuania and Poland 32.7. The paper's claim was wrong and has been reversed; the enrolment figure was reconciled (see Q11).

- Before: "The difference was not one of social capability: Latvian literacy was above Finnish and Latvian university enrolment per head, 4.3 per thousand in the 1930s, nearly double the Western core's \parencite{klimantas2025thesis}."
- After: "The difference was not primarily one of social capability: Latvian literacy, though below Finnish (11 per cent of Latvians aged ten and over were illiterate in 1935, under 1 per cent of Finns in 1930), was high for the European periphery, and Latvian university enrolment per head, 4.3 per thousand in the 1930s (30 per 10,000 in Kalniņš's contemporary comparison, the highest in the Baltic), was nearly double the Western core's \parencites{klimantas2025thesis}[102--3]{norkusdvides}."
- Key/page/quote: norkusdvides [102--3]; "Suomija 1930 15 0,9 … Latvija 1935 10 11,1"; "studentų skaičius … dešimčiai tūkstančių gyventojų … (atitinkamai 15,0; 28,8; 30,1)". "Not primarily" replaces "not" because the argument that follows (resources and markets) no longer rests on a literacy lead.

**C2. Serfdom in Latvia 1816--19 (L33).** The statement held only for Courland and Livonia; in Latgale (Vitebsk governorate) serfdom ended in 1861. Added "(outside Latgale)".

- Before: "serfdom had ended in Latvia and Estonia in 1816--19, two generations before Russia"
- After: "serfdom had ended in Latvia (outside Latgale) and Estonia in 1816--19, two generations before Russia … \parencites[30]{clemens_2003_comparative}[358]{norkusdvides}"
- Key/page/quote: clemens_2003_comparative [30]: "Abolition of serfdom in Estonia, Courland, and Livonia in 1816-1819, long before emancipation in Latgale, Lithuania, and Russia proper in 1861". (Also grigoriadis_vitola_2025 p. 354: Estland 1816, Courland 1817, Livonia 1819; Lettgallia in the Vitebsk Governorate; not cited.)

## (d) Checked but not changed / not resolved

1. **Estonia's kroon "1928" (L41).** Paper says gold-backed currencies were introduced in 1922 (LV, LT) and 1928 (EE). The kroon was indeed introduced on 1 January 1928; Norkus's "1924" refers to the de facto stabilisation of the Estonian mark (Aldcroft & Morewood 2013 Table 2.3, p. 63: Estonia "1924(1927)", Latvia "1921"; opened and verified). No change; the sentence already carries three sources, so no citation added.
2. **Norkus et al. 2025 p. 6 gives Estonia's recovery of 1913 income as 1924, the paper 1925** (companion series). Cited only for Latvia 1930; the one-year difference is noted here, not in the text.
3. **March 1949 deportations: older Western estimates differ** (Misiunas & Taagepera p. 99: ≥50,000 LV, 40,000 LT, "60,000" EE; Hiden & Salmon p. 129: 136,000 LV 1944--52). Archival counts (Feest pp. 99--100) match the paper; rule 3 applied, older figures not added.
4. **"Flight of some 250,000 people to the West in 1944" (L51):** no page in the corpus gives this Baltic-wide figure (King: 200,000 emigrants from Latvia 1939--49; M&T p. 59: 20,000 Latvians reached the West from German formations). Not cited.
5. **"Lithuania, where 80 per cent of the population still lived on the land in 1946" (L57):** nearest figures are urban shares of 39 per cent in 1959 (Zunde p. 167) and 34.6 per cent in 1955 (Dremaite p. 241); no 1946 figure found. Not cited.
6. **"The first warning came in 1955 … halved" and the 1962 recession (L59):** own series; M&T p. 189 note only a 1960s slowdown and no source describes a 1962 downturn (the Soviet-period sheet confirms). Nothing added.
