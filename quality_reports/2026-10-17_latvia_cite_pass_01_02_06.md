## (b) Corrections made

1. **02_gap, Rosés and Wolf sentence.**
   - Before: "which rest on an undocumented procedure and make interwar Latvia Europe's fastest-growing economy and, by 1938, richer than Finland."
   - After: "which rest on an undocumented procedure and make interwar Latvia the fastest-growing economy in Europe outside the Soviet Union and, by 1938, richer than Finland, estimates the authors themselves concede `might be on the high side'."
   - Key, page: `roseswolf2010` [188, 190] (already cited as [187--90] in the sentence).
   - Quote: Table 8.2, 1929--38 growth: "Latvia 4.10 (0.12) ... Russia/USSR 4.87 (0.05)"; 1920--29: "Latvia 5.31 (0.11)" (the highest of the twenty-seven listed, USSR n/a) (p. 188); "the positive development of Latvia and Estonia (while the estimates here might be on the high side)" (p. 190).
   - Reason: on their own table the USSR grows faster than Latvia in 1929--38, so "Europe's fastest-growing economy" without qualification misreports the source; Latvia is the fastest among the non-Soviet economies over both sub-periods. Their own caveat is added because it bears directly on the paper's criticism.

No other sentence in the three sections was found to be contradicted by a page text.

## (c) Qualifications and short clauses added

1. **02_gap, footnote to the Rosés--Wolf sentence** (qualification of the earlier estimate): records that Rosés and Wolf attribute the Baltic recovery to an "early exit from the gold standard with the UK in late 1931" (`roseswolf2010` [189]) when Latvia imposed exchange control in October 1931 and left gold only on 28 September 1936 (`bernankegd01` [74]; `richter_2026_economic` [146]). Strengthens the paper's criticism of their Baltic figures; no claim of the paper changed.
2. **02_gap, Aizsilnieks dispute**: clause "a reading Karnups has lately renewed \parencite[145, 148--9]{karnups_2017_karla}" added after Aizsilnieks's position, and `krumins_2017_laika_lidz` [26] attached to "the Latvian historians who regarded those years as prosperous". The dispute is thereby shown to be alive inside current Latvian historiography (Karnups: national income "grew slowly", 1929 level regained only in 1938; Krūmiņš: Ulmanis's rule "coincided with a period of economic upswing"). Meaning of the sentence unchanged.
3. **02_gap, footnote to "Latvia was not Europe's fastest-growing economy in 1929--38"** (case 2, different period and comparison set): Bukowski, Kowalski and Wroński, using the same Latvian series, call Latvia "the region's and Europe's leader of economic growth" for 1924--38 at 3.4 per cent a year; their comparison set excludes Finland and the Soviet Union (`bukowski_2026_economic` [3, 19--20]). The paper's claim is left intact.
4. **02_gap, footnote to the net-recipient sentence** (case 2): the conclusion that Latvia was a net contributor is contested by `aage_1997_public` [102] (implicit energy subsidies imply positive net transfers), a point `viksnins_1986_latvian` [252] had noted depends on the price assigned to energy; `samonis_1995_transforming` [6--7] answers that the net flow ran outwards. Vaškela 2014 [232--3] added in-text as a third page-referenced source for the paper's position (Baltic share of all-Union investment 2.19 per cent in 1946--60, below its share of production).
5. **02_gap, added clause on the official industrial index**: "it is, moreover, pegged to a 1940 base that covers only the five months of Soviet rule, and in physical units the 1937 level had not been regained by 1947" (`years_of_dependence` [109]). Factual addition supporting the paper's criticism of the official index (29 words).
6. **02_gap, added clause on the MPD 1989 rise**: "a rise inherited from the official series and reproduced in the EBRD's compilations" (`kyn_1997_eastern` [269]; `kukk_2014_road` [13]). Registers that the anomaly is not peculiar to the Maddison Project (14 words).
7. **02_gap, added clause after Koropeckyj**: "as does Fedotov's claim of 1988 that Latvian national income per head exceeded 70 per cent of the American level" (`kukk2005` [166]). Adds the Soviet-side counterpart to the Western overestimate (21 words).

## (d) Claims looked for but not supported ("not resolved")

1. **01_intro, "the richest of them in 1913, Latvia"**: the Dresdner Bank (1930) estimates of national income per head at 1913--14 prices put Estonia (304 RM) above Latvia (274 RM) (fact sheet batch03, `norkusdvides` pp. 94--5; Norkus attributes the low Latvian average to Latgale). The paper's 1913 benchmark (Norkus and Markevičiūtė 2021, reproduced in `norkus_2023_output` p. 129) supports the paper. A known disagreement between sources rather than an error; no change, no footnote. Pages 94--5 were not opened, so not cited.
2. **02_gap, Aizsilnieks's own statement that national income per head did not grow under Ulmanis**: `krumins_2024_makroekonomiska` p. 77 reports Aizsilnieks's finding that industrial net output per worker fell by over a fifth in 1934--38 (not national income per head); `norkus_2016_comparison` p. 5 confirms that Aizsilnieks (1968: 834) is the only émigré source with national-income data for 1934--38 but does not state his conclusion. Neither page says exactly what the paper attributes to him, so no citation was attached to Aizsilnieks's position itself (Karnups was cited instead for the same reading).
3. **02_gap, "Neither the Baltic statistical offices nor Eurostat, the IMF or the World Bank publish GDP for the Baltic states before 1990"**: the only corpus evidence (`cerniauskas_2011_emerging` p. 374) concerns Lithuania and the years 1990--94; not cited.
4. **02_gap, "The Maddison Project Database reports Latvian GDP per capita for 1973 and annually from 1980"**: `norkusdvides` p. 71 gives the 1973 Maddison figure but describes an older release ("only 1973 and 1990"); to avoid confusing database versions no citation was added (`mpd2023` is already cited in 01_intro for the same statement).
5. **02_gap, the Soviet "net recipient" claim itself**: `dumbliauskas_2008_sovietines` pp. 12--13 documents the Soviet propaganda that the Baltic republics "had to accept 'unselfish and generous aid'"; not added because the sentence already carries three sources and the evidence is framed around Lithuania.
6. **06_conclusion, "Latvia lost more than a third of its population ... in the First World War"**: supported by `norkusdvides` p. 335 ("Of the 2.5 million inhabitants ... about 1,580 thousand remained"), but left uncited in the conclusion, which is meant to carry few citations; the devastation claim is cited in 01_intro.
7. **"Soviet West" attribution** (`demuth_2000_politics` p. 173; `pravatorau_2024_soviet` pp. 29--30): no sentence in 01_intro, 02_gap or 06_conclusion uses the phrase ("our little West" in the intro is already cited to Kodres), so nothing was added.
8. **01_intro, Finland-parity sentence**: `krumins_2024_makroekonomiska` p. 60 ("lagging Finland ... by a mere 10%") is a further current statement of near-parity but was not added to keep the sentence at three sources.
9. **06_conclusion, Vonyó and Klein**: page numbers could not be given reliably (pre-print pagination in the corpus); cited without pages.
